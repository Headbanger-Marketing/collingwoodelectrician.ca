EV charger installation in Collingwood.

Installing an EV charger at a Collingwood house can raise ownership questions, especially where a tenant charges and a landlord owns the property. Agree who owns the charger and circuit and what happens when the tenancy ends.

The charger is only one part of the installation

An EV charging project includes equipment, a suitable connection and an assessment of the property supply. ESA advises using approved charging equipment, a dedicated circuit and outdoor-rated equipment where needed. The contractor should evaluate the proposed load and explain whether the supply, a load-management arrangement or another design is suitable. Do not choose a circuit from the charger advertising alone.

Prepare the scope for your Collingwood property

Write down the parking position, the route from the panel and who holds the lease or ownership of that space. Photograph the spot and the panel door from outside, and collect any charger instructions already chosen.

Ask the contractor whether the landlord's written approval should name the location, whether the circuit can stay if the charger is removed, and what removal would involve if the tenant moves out.

What should the assessment establish?

Who owns the charger and the circuit when a tenant charges and a landlord owns the property? The scope names the owner of each, along with the landlord's written consent and the ESA notification. Is the existing supply enough for it? The capacity check, with the contractor's account of what was examined rather than an assumption that the supply is sufficient, notes whether a dedicated circuit or outdoor-rated equipment is needed. Who pays for the circuit if the charger later comes out? That cost has a named payer, and should the Collingwood rental be sold, the scope says whether the charger stays.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance, and EV charging safety guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Does a landlord have to consent before a tenant adds a charger?

The answer rests on the lease and on what the landlord agrees, so settle it in writing before equipment is bought. Ask the contractor whether the written consent should be attached to the scope of work.

What happens to the charger when the tenant moves out?

The agreement should say whether the charger is removed, left in place or sold on, and who restores the wall or post. Without that, a sale or new tenancy can start with an unclear item.
