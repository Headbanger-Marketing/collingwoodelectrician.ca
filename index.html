Before work begins

Give each item a reason before it is repaired or replaced.

Getting your home ready to sell? The listing date can limit how much work fits, and a buyer's inspector may ask what changed and why, so each item needs a reason you can explain. Without a sale, those reasons can still guide each choice.

Before any work is priced, list each electrical item you are unsure about and note whether it works, looks worn or has been altered. Keep invoices, ESA paperwork and product sheets together so the contractor can see what exists.

Which paperwork helps a contractor judge what to fix or keep?

Collect earlier ESA paperwork, invoices, product sheets and any inspection report, and note who did past work. A contractor can use them to judge which items need attention and which can stay as they are. Records do not replace an assessment.

Why might two quotes propose different work on one item?

Contractors may weigh repair, replacement and leaving alone differently. Ask each to state, item by item, which of the three applies and why, then compare the stated reasons before comparing the totals.

Is replacing a worn device always better than repairing it?

Not always. A device that works, is intact and suits its use may be left alone, while one that is damaged, hot or repeatedly failing should be assessed by a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Request the written reasoning for each choice.

Who needs to approve electrical work before a house is listed?

That depends on who owns and occupies the property and on any agreements in place. Name the person who can authorize the work and open the doors, and ask the contractor which approvals and ESA notification apply.

Does sending the form arrange a visit or a contract with anyone?

No. The site informs and does not arrange visits, set prices or do electrical work; a form sent here is received and reviewed as an enquiry. A provider or Licensed Electrical Contractor confirms who it is and its licence separately and agrees scope, cost and timing with you; check the licence with ESA before agreeing.
